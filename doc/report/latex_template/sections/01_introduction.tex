\section{Introduction}
Whether out of scientific interest, curiosity, or to raise awareness of space-related risks, the observation of near-Earth objects is now a topic of significant interest. Near-Earth Objects (NEOs) refer to asteroids and comets whose orbits can bring them close to Earth. Because of the potentially consequences of an impact by such an object, their detection and tracking are subject to constant monitoring, particularly by NASA. NASA makes a large amount of data on NEOs available to the public, accessible notably through its API.

\vspace{0.25}

However, despite the richness of this data, using it directly is not necessarily tailored to a user’s needs. Quickly identifying near-Earth objects, searching for a specific NEO, tracking its trajectory over time, or receiving alerts about events of potential interest requires tools that can process and present this information in a more accessible way. Access to the data alone is therefore not enough. It is necessary to provide an interface that allows users to view, organize, and customize the data according to their individual needs.
NEO-Watch is part of this initiative, an app designed to facilitate access to and tracking of near-Earth objects using data provided by NASA.

\vspace{0.25}

This project therefore prompts us to consider the design of an application capable of leveraging external data while meeting specific needs in terms of search, customization, and tracking. This analysis report first presents the project’s specifications, as well as its scope, features, requirements, and the choices made. The second part is devoted to the team’s organization and work planning. The third part presents the application’s overall design, including various diagrams, the chosen architecture, the data model, and the main technical components. Finally, the last part details the components to be implemented and the estimated timeframes for their completion, before concluding with a summary of the project.
